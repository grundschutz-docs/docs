The catalog says **what** an organization should implement. The methodology says **how** to get there: in what order, with which decisions, and when to look closer. The BSI has published it as **BSI Standard 300-1 "Methodik Grundschutz++"** ([press release of 26 October 2026](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Service-Navi/Presse/Alle-Meldungen-News/Meldungen/2026/BSI-Standard_300-1_261026.html), in German). The PDF is version 1.0, and its change history gives 20 October 2026 as the date.

According to the standard, version 1.0 is aimed first of all at **early adopters**: experienced organizations that want to start early. Their experience is meant to feed into further development (1). An information security management system (ISMS) built on Grundschutz++ is planned to be [certifiable from 2027](/grundschutzpp/zeitplan/).

## The five steps

The methodology describes a PDCA cycle (Plan, Do, Check, Act) with five process steps. Each step is itself run iteratively (1). Figure 1 of the standard assigns them like this: **Plan** is steps 1 and 2, **Do** is step 3, **Check** is step 4 and **Act** is step 5.

<MethodikKreislauf lang="en" />

- **1 Survey and planning** (chapter 2 of the standard, ISO/IEC 27001 clauses 4 and 5): [GC](/grundschutzpp/gc/) Governance and compliance
- **2 Requirements analysis** (chapter 3, ISO/IEC 27001 clauses 6 and 10): [STM](/grundschutzpp/stm/) Structure modelling
- **3 Realization** (chapter 4): [UMS](/grundschutzpp/ums/) Implementation, [RISK](/grundschutzpp/risk/) Risk management
- **4 Monitoring** (chapter 5): [PERF](/grundschutzpp/perf/) Monitoring and evaluation
- **5 Continual improvement** (chapter 6): [VRB](/grundschutzpp/vrb/) Improvement

The standard names ISO clauses explicitly only for steps 1 and 2. The practice after the colon is **my own mapping** by topic and title, not a statement by the BSI. Only the grouping of the practices into three groups comes from the standard (figure 2): **methodology** (GC, STM, UMS, VRB, PERF, RISK), **technical** (ARCH, BER, DET, REA, KONF, DEV) and **organizational** (ASST, PERS, BES, DLS, TEST, GEB, SENS, NOT).

## Step 1: survey and planning

Top management decides what the ISMS covers and what it does not (2). That decision is theirs: responsibility for the ISMS lies with top management and cannot be delegated (2.10). The standard names these sub-steps, each followed by the catalog requirements that fit it:

- Context of the organization (2.1): [GC.2.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.2.1), [GC.2.2](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.2.2)
- Involve compliance management (2.2): [GC.3.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.3.1)
- Scope (2.3): [GC.6.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.6.1)
- Interested parties (2.4): [GC.4.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.4.1), [GC.4.2](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.4.2)
- Business processes (2.5): [GC.7.1.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.7.1.1)
- Protection needs (2.6): [GC.7.1.2](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.7.1.2)
- Information security policy (2.7): [GC.5.1.3](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.5.1.3), [GC.5.1.4](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.5.1.4)
- Roles and responsibilities (2.8): [GC.9.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.9.1)
- Communication and documentation (2.9): [GC.10.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.10.1), [GC.11.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.11.1)
- Approval by top management (2.10): [GC.1.2](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.1.2), [GC.8.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.8.1)
- Prepare the risk assessment (2.11): [GC.12.1](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.12.1)

### Protection needs: only two levels

Each business process gets a protection need for confidentiality, integrity and availability. There are **two categories** (2.6):

- **normal** (*normal*): the impact is limited and manageable.
- **high** (*hoch*): the impact can be considerable.

You may define additional categories of your own, but they have to be mapped onto these two. For a certifiable ISMS, the lowest category must always meet the state of the art.

If you are setting up an ISMS for the first time, the standard says to start with the most important business process in the highest category and then work through the rest in turn.

**Protection needs decide whether a risk assessment is required.** With normal protection needs you do not have to carry out one of your own, because the catalog is already based on a general BSI risk assessment (2.11). With high protection needs, one is required. More on that below.

## Step 2: requirements analysis

This step turns the catalog into an **individual requirements package** (*Anforderungspaket*) for your information domain (3.4). The way there:

1. **Delimit the information domain** (3.1), [STM.1.1](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.1.1) and [STM.1.2](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.1.2). The scope sets the formal extent of the ISMS; the information domain maps it in terms of content and technology.
2. **Record the assets** (3.2), [STM.2.1.2](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.2). For high protection needs you must document which assets belong to the business process and how they depend on each other. For normal protection needs, document at least the dependencies between applications, IT systems and rooms. Figures 3 and 4 show the chains: for high protection needs business process, application, IT system, network or network component, room. For normal protection needs it is application to IT system and IT system to room.
3. **Map assets to target object categories** (3.4.2), [STM.2.1.3](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.3). These are standardized classes such as "web servers", "cloud services" or "server rooms". Annex 1 of the standard defines 39 of them, and the BSI also provides them as a [CSV file](https://github.com/BSI-Bund/Stand-der-Technik-Bibliothek/blob/main/documentation/namespaces/target_object_categories.csv). They form a hierarchy of seven root nodes and four levels (figure 6): an asset also receives the categories above its own ([STM.2.1.4.1](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.4.1)).
4. **Consider blueprints** (3.3). A blueprint is a ready-made catalog of requirements for a particular scenario, with predefined parameters. For some organizations there may be mandatory blueprints.
5. **Add requirements** (3.4.3) if an asset is not covered by the catalog or if duties arise from compliance or the stakeholder analysis: [STM.2.1.6](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.6) and [STM.2.1.7](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.7).
6. **Set parameters** (3.5), [STM.5.1](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.5.1). Parameters are the variable parts of a requirement, such as "regularly". The organization makes them concrete.

The package contains three kinds of requirements (3.4):

- **Methodology requirements** apply to every information domain and are mandatory. They sit in the methodology practices (GC, STM, UMS, VRB, PERF, RISK) and reflect ISO 27001 clauses 4 to 10. "An individual approach is not intended" (3.4.1). In the catalog that is **99** requirements.
- **Requirements without a target object category** from the technical and organizational practices are likewise mandatory ([STM.2.1.5](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.5)). In the catalog that is **265**.
- **Requirements with a target object category** apply as far as you have assets in that category ([STM.2.1.4](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.4)). In the catalog, **636** requirements carry the category in a field called `target_object_categories`. Together with the other two groups that makes the 1,000 requirements.

A requirement that affects several assets of the same category enters the package only once ([STM.2.1.4.2](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.2.1.4.2)).

### MUSS, SOLLTE, KANN and the two security levels

Every requirement carries a modal verb and a security level. In the catalog (version 22 October 2026, 1,000 requirements) the split is:

| Modal verb | Count | Level |
|---|---|---|
| MUSS (must) | 149 | normal-SdT |
| SOLLTE (should) | 624 | normal-SdT |
| SOLLTE (should) | 3 | erhöht (elevated) |
| KANN (may) | 224 | erhöht (elevated) |

On the levels, the standard says (3.4.2): requirements at the **elevated** level contain the modal verb KANN and are optional. You can use them to find further measures where the risk is elevated.

The three SOLLTE requirements at the elevated level are [BES.1.4](/grundschutzpp/bes/#BES.1.4), [DET.4.12.1](/grundschutzpp/det/#DET.4.12.1) and [REA.2.5](/grundschutzpp/rea/#REA.2.5). That does not quite match the standard's sentence. If you filter by level, keep them in mind.

## Step 3: realization

### Determine the implementation status

The status of **every** requirement in the package has to be checked ([UMS.1.1](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.1.1)). The standard knows three values (4.1.1): "fulfilled", "not fulfilled" and "not applicable". "Not applicable" needs a documented reason, for example when the underlying component does not exist in the information domain.

The catalog puts it more briefly: the status is basically "implemented (yes)" or "not implemented (no)", and a requirement counts as implemented only if all requirements it depends on are implemented too. "Not applicable" is not mentioned there. In practice it helps to read both texts together.

### What happens when a requirement is not met

If a requirement is not yet met but already in the implementation plan, the risk that is not yet covered must be identified and reported to top management, which has to bear it (4.1.1). Everything else depends on the modal verb (4.1.2):

- **KANN:** optional, need not be considered.
- **SOLLTE, temporarily not met:** fulfilment is planned for later. A sound justification is enough. This does not trigger a risk assessment.
- **SOLLTE, permanently not met:** meaning "for the foreseeable future or for good". Then a **risk assessment** is mandatory, and the residual risk goes to top management for approval.
- **MUSS:** mandatory. A risk assessment **cannot replace** fulfilment. According to the standard, an exemption or risk acceptance is **not possible**.

The standard warns that if permanent non-fulfilments of SOLLTE requirements pile up, the state of the art as a whole may count as not reached, so a certification could find a significant deviation.

In the catalog, [UMS.5.1](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.5.1) is titled "Authorization of exceptions". It refers to exemptions where obligations conflict. Both statements apply side by side.

### Plan the implementation

The requirements package becomes an implementation plan ([UMS.2.1](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.2.1), [UMS.2.2](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.2.2)) (4.3). For each measure, record at least the related requirement, the date of implementation or review, and the person who documents or checks it. If checks are automated, record the tool, the time and the result.

Add owners and deadlines ([UMS.3.1](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.3.1), [UMS.4.1](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.4.1)) (4.4) and progress tracking ([UMS.6.1](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.6.1), [UMS.6.2](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.6.2)) (4.5). The standard recommends implementing the requirements of parent nodes in the category hierarchy first, because they are mostly conceptual ("establish a procedure").

## When a risk assessment is mandatory

The catalog already contains a **general risk assessment** based on an assumed typical threat situation. The underlying threats are stored as metadata on the requirements. In the catalog, 901 of 1,000 requirements carry such data, covering 47 distinct elementary threats (Annex 4). With normal protection needs, this is all you need.

An **explicit risk assessment** is required in these cases, according to the standard (2.11):

1. A business process has **high protection needs** for at least one protection goal ([GC.7.2](/grundschutzpp/gc/#GC.7.2)).
2. A relevant asset is **not adequately covered** by the target object categories and their requirements.
3. An applicable SOLLTE requirement is **permanently not met**.

In [STM.4.1](/grundschutzpp/stm/#STM.4.1) the catalog names a further trigger, the **downgrading of the security level** from elevated to normal-SdT. The standard's list does not include this case.

For **residual risks** of SOLLTE requirements that are in the implementation plan but not yet met, a residual risk assessment is required and top management must approve the risk ([UMS.1.2](/grundschutzpp/ums/#UMS.1.2)).

### The method is free, the minimum criteria are not

You choose how to identify, assess and treat risks, for example following ISO/IEC 27005 or ISO 31000 (2.11). The **35 minimum criteria** in Annex 3 are binding, so that the result stays comparable and verifiable, for instance in a certification. Some of them:

- a uniform, documented procedure for identification, assessment and treatment (criteria 1 to 4)
- a defined **risk appetite** and risk acceptance criteria (7, 8)
- a **risk owner** for every risk, by default the business process owner ([RISK.1.3](/grundschutzpp/risk/#RISK.1.3)) (5, 16)
- a risk register with fixed minimum fields (15)
- authorization of procedures, criteria and residual risks by top management ([RISK.1.5](/grundschutzpp/risk/#RISK.1.5)) (9, 30)
- review at least **annually** and on significant changes (35)

## Steps 4 and 5: monitor and improve

The standard requires (5):

- a **performance evaluation** of the ISMS (5.1). According to the standard, uniform rules for it are still to come, and until then organizations are free. In the [timeline](/grundschutzpp/zeitplan/) the BSI gives 26 February 2027 for the performance measurement concept.
- an **internal audit programme** (5.2), meant above all to show room for improvement
- **management reviews** by top management (5.3)
- **monitoring** with procedures for security incidents, vulnerabilities and new threats (5.4)
- **validation of the requirements**, for example after changes to the information domain or the catalog (5.5)

Continual improvement (6) closes the loop: analyse non-conformities, assess improvement potential, add a correction and improvement plan to the implementation plan, check effectiveness.
